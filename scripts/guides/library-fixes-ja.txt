=== add phrase.ja.に関して | word | に関して / に関しては (regarding; as for)
## What it means
"Regarding, concerning, about." に関して is a compound particle: it marks the topic something is about. With は on the end, に関しては means "as for, when it comes to". That's the form in your line.

- About, concerning:
この{件|けん}に{関|かん}して{質問|しつもん}があります。 — I have a question about this matter.
- As for (with は, singling out the topic, often in contrast with others):
{費用|ひよう}に{関|かん}しては、{後|あと}で{説明|せつめい}します。 — As for the cost, I'll explain later.
- Before a noun, as に関する:
{事件|じけん}に{関|かん}する{記録|きろく} — records concerning the case

## How it's built
- に (target) + {関|かん}して, the te-form of {関|かん}する "to be connected with". Literally "being connected to X", so "about X".
- On its own, 関する is rare. It lives almost entirely in these fixed forms: に関して, に関しては, に関しても, に関する + noun. Learn it as the phrase, not as a verb.
- Extra-polite version: に{関|かん}しまして (announcements, speeches).

## Where it fits
Formal and neutral language: reports, news, the police, business, explanations. In casual conversation people usually say について.
- Xに関して + verb: {調査|ちょうさ}に{関|かん}して{話|はな}す — talk about the investigation
- Xに関しては: one topic set apart: {他|ほか}の{件|けん}はともかく、この{件|けん}に{関|かん}しては… — whatever about the other cases, as for this one…

## Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- について: the same meaning, more everyday. Use it in casual talk.
{日本|にほん}について{話|はな}そう。 — Let's talk about Japan. (に関して would sound like a meeting.)
- に{対|たい}して: "toward, against, in response to". It's directed at someone, not "about" them.
{先生|せんせい}に{対|たい}して{失礼|しつれい}だ。 — That's rude toward the teacher.
- に{関|かか}わる: "to affect, have a bearing on". Same kanji, different reading and meaning.
{命|いのち}に{関|かか}わる{病気|びょうき} — a life-threatening illness

## Nuance and register
Formal, precise, a little official. In an interrogation it sounds like the record speaking: the detective is going through what's on file.

## Common mistakes
- Treating 関する as an ordinary verb and conjugating it freely (関しない, 関した are unnatural).
- Using に関して in casual chat, where について sounds natural.
- Confusing it with に対して ("toward").

## Where you met it
ことビルカー{投資|とうし}{銀行|ぎんこう}のオーベルト{氏|し}{殺害|さつがい}に{関|かん}しては — "when it comes to the murder of Mr. Obert of Birker Investment Bank specifically…". に関しては makes the murder the topic, and the こと in front singles it out from the other cases (see the guide to こと + noun + に関しては).

=== add ja.koto_noun_ni_kanshite | grammar | こと + noun + に関しては (when it comes to X specifically)
## What it is
こと placed directly in front of a noun, followed by に関しては, となると or については, means "when it comes specifically to X (unlike other things)". It draws a line around one topic and treats it as the special case or the exception.

## How to make it
- こと + noun + に{関|かん}しては: こと{お金|おかね}に{関|かん}しては — when it comes to money specifically
- こと + noun + となると / になると: こと{料理|りょうり}となると、{彼|かれ}は{厳|きび}しい。 — When it comes to cooking, he's strict.
- こと + noun + にかけては (for skills): こと{数学|すうがく}にかけては{誰|だれ}にも{負|ま}けない。 — When it comes to maths, he's second to none.
The こと goes straight before the noun, with no particle after it.

## When to use it
- Contrast: other matters are one way, this one is different.
{他|ほか}のことはともかく、こと{勉強|べんきょう}に{関|かん}しては{真面目|まじめ}だ。 — Whatever else, when it comes to studying, he's serious.
- A special strength or obsession (often にかけては or となると).

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- With no contrast or emphasis intended, plain に関しては or について is enough. こと adds "this one specifically".
- This こと is not the noun "matter" or the nominalizer in {泳|およ}ぐこと. It belongs to the noun after it. Don't read ことビルカー as part of a name.

## Nuance and register
Emphatic and slightly formal. In speech it sounds pointed: the speaker is setting one topic apart on purpose.

## Common mistakes
- Putting a particle after こと (ことのお金 ✗).
- Using it without any sense of "unlike the others".

## Where you met it
その{他|た}の{件|けん}では あれほど{少女|しょうじょ}たちの{調査|ちょうさ}をしていた{君|きみ}だ。 then ことビルカー{投資|とうし}{銀行|ぎんこう}のオーベルト{氏|し}{殺害|さつがい}に{関|かん}しては… — "In the other cases you investigated the girls so thoroughly. But when it comes to the Obert murder specifically…". こと sets that one murder apart: the detective is pointing out that this case doesn't fit the pattern.

=== section word.ja.こと | Where you met it
In ことビルカー{投資|とうし}{銀行|ぎんこう}のオーベルト{氏|し}{殺害|さつがい}に{関|かん}しては, こと is not a garbled subtitle or part of a name. It's the こと of the pattern こと + noun + に関しては, "when it comes to X specifically": the detective has just said how thoroughly the other cases were investigated, and こと sets the Obert murder apart from them. See the guide to こと + noun + に関しては.

=== add phrase.ja.歯切れが悪い | word | 歯切れが悪い (evasive, not speaking plainly)
## What it means
"Evasive, vague, not answering plainly": said of someone who hedges instead of giving a clear answer. Literally "the cut of the teeth is bad".

- Hedging, dodging a straight answer:
{質問|しつもん}されると、{社長|しゃちょう}は{急|きゅう}に{歯切|はぎ}れが{悪|わる}くなった。 — When questioned, the president suddenly turned evasive.
- Of an answer or explanation:
{歯切|はぎ}れの{悪|わる}い{返事|へんじ} — a vague, hedging reply
- The opposite is 歯切れがいい (crisp, clear-cut, decisive):
{歯切|はぎ}れのいい{話|はな}し{方|かた} — a crisp, clear way of talking

## How it's built
{歯|は} "teeth" + {切|き}れ "cut" (from {切|き}れる) gives {歯切|はぎ}れ (the き becomes ぎ in the compound), how cleanly something is bitten through. It's still used of food: このせんべいは{歯切|はぎ}れがいい "this cracker has a nice crisp bite". Add が{悪|わる}い "is bad" and the image is words that don't come out cleanly. Learn 歯切れが悪い and 歯切れがいい as units.

## Where it fits
Commenting on how someone is answering: journalists about politicians, detectives about suspects, a friend who notices you're dodging. Usually an observation about someone else.
- With ようだね / みたいだね it becomes a calm remark: {歯切|はぎ}れが{悪|わる}いね — You're being cagey.

## Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- {曖昧|あいまい}な: "vague, ambiguous" about the content itself (an ambiguous rule). 歯切れが悪い is about the way someone answers.
- {口|くち}ごもる: "to falter, mumble": stumbling over the words themselves.
- ごまかす: "to fudge, cover up": deliberate. 歯切れが悪い only says the answer isn't clean.
- {滑舌|かつぜつ}が{悪|わる}い: unclear pronunciation (mumbling, tripping over sounds), not evasiveness.

## Nuance and register
Neutral to slightly formal, mildly critical. Said to someone's face, it's pointed: it tells them you've noticed.

## Common mistakes
- Using it for pronunciation problems (that's 滑舌が悪い).
- Learning 歯切れ on its own; it almost always comes with がいい or が悪い.

## Where you met it
{歯切|はぎ}れが{悪|わる}いようだね — "You seem rather evasive." The interrogator notices the suspect hedging. ようだね turns it into a calm observation, but there's a needle in it: the evasiveness has been noticed.

=== add phrase.ja.専らの評判 | word | 専らの評判だ (it's what everyone says)
## What it means
"Everyone says so; it's the general talk": a fixed expression saying a rumour or opinion is widespread.

- {彼|かれ}が{次|つぎ}の{社長|しゃちょう}だというのが{専|もっぱ}らの{評判|ひょうばん}だ。 — It's widely said that he'll be the next president.
- Same meaning: {専|もっぱ}らの{噂|うわさ}だ。

## How it's built
{専|もっぱ}ら "mainly, almost exclusively" + の + {評判|ひょうばん} "reputation, talk": "the talk that's nearly all there is". It often follows a quoted clause: ～というのが専らの評判だ, ～と専らの評判だ.

## Where it fits
Reported gossip in a slightly formal, narrating tone: news, detectives, storytellers summing up what people say.

## Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- ～らしい / ～そうだ (hearsay): reports what you heard; 専らの評判 says everyone is saying it.
- {有名|ゆうめい}だ: "famous": about the person or thing, not a rumour.
- 専ら on its own is an adverb, "mostly": {休日|きゅうじつ}は{専|もっぱ}ら{寝|ね}ている。 — On days off I mostly sleep.

## Nuance and register
Slightly literary. It sounds like a narrator, or someone summing up the gossip.

## Common mistakes
Reading 専ら as せんら. It's もっぱら.

## Where you met it
{専|もっぱ}らの{評判|ひょうばん}だ — "That's what everyone's saying." It closes the gossip being reported about the girl and the gymnasium boys: not one person's claim, but the general talk.

=== add phrase.ja.実行に移す | word | 実行に移す (put into action)
## What it means
"To put into action, carry out (a plan)": the step from deciding to actually doing.

- {計画|けいかく}を{実行|じっこう}に{移|うつ}す。 — put the plan into action
- {考|かんが}えを{実行|じっこう}に{移|うつ}すのは{難|むずか}しい。 — Turning ideas into action is hard.

## How it's built
{実行|じっこう} "carrying out" + に "into" + {移|うつ}す "to move (something)": "move it into execution". The plan takes を, often dropped when it's clear from context.

## Where it fits
Plans, ideas, threats, resolutions. Neutral and slightly formal; common in narration and the news.

## Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- {実行|じっこう}する: plain "carry out", without the sense of crossing from plan to deed.
- {行動|こうどう}に{移|うつ}す: "act on" feelings or ideas: {思|おも}いを{行動|こうどう}に{移|うつ}す.
- やる / する: everyday "do".

## Nuance and register
Weighty: it marks a decisive moment. In a confession it sounds chillingly matter-of-fact.

## Common mistakes
Using 移る (intransitive): the idiom is 移す.

## Where you met it
で {俺|おれ}は {実行|じっこう}に{移|うつ}した — "So I put it into action." で ("so, and then") moves the story on: the plan he has just described becomes the deed, said flatly.

=== add phrase.ja.悪態をつく | word | 悪態をつく (curse, swear)
## What it means
"To curse, swear, mutter abuse": spit out insults, often at no one in particular or behind someone's back.

- {彼|かれ}は{上司|じょうし}の{背中|せなか}に{悪態|あくたい}をついた。 — He cursed at his boss's back.

## How it's built
{悪態|あくたい} "abusive words" + をつく. This つく means "let out", the same つく as in {嘘|うそ}をつく "tell a lie" and ため{息|いき}をつく "sigh". Learn it as a unit.

## Where it fits
Narration and description of someone venting: cursing fate, a situation, someone absent.

## Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- {悪口|わるぐち}を{言|い}う: badmouth someone (to other people).
- {罵|ののし}る: abuse someone to their face, harsher.
- {文句|もんく}を{言|い}う: complain.

## Nuance and register
Descriptive and a little literary; people describe others (or their past selves) doing it.

## Common mistakes
悪態を言う ✗. The verb is つく.

## Where you met it
いつもの{独|ひと}り{言|ごと}のように{俺|おれ}は{悪態|あくたい}をついた — "I cursed, the way I always talk to myself." He vents abuse at no one, out of habit.

=== add phrase.ja.店を出す | word | 店を出す (open a shop; set up in business)
## What it means
"To open a shop, set up one's own business": start running a shop or restaurant. It doesn't mean opening the doors for the day.

- {駅前|えきまえ}に{店|みせ}を{出|だ}した。 — opened a shop in front of the station
- At a festival: {屋台|やたい}を{出|だ}す — set up a stall

## How it's built
{店|みせ} + を + {出|だ}す "put out": "put a shop out into the world". 出す is used for launching things publicly: {本|ほん}を{出|だ}す "publish a book".

## Where it fits
Everyday and neutral. In gossip it suggests someone has set themselves up on their own.

## Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- {店|みせ}を{開|あ}ける: open the shop for the day (unlock the doors).
- {開店|かいてん}する: formal "open a shop", also used for opening time.
- {店|みせ}をたたむ: close a business for good. {店|みせ}を{閉|し}める: close for the day (or for good).
- {開業|かいぎょう}する: start any business, formal.

## Nuance and register
Plain and everyday.

## Common mistakes
Using 店を出す for "open today". That's 開ける.

## Where you met it
たんまり{金|かね}をもらって{店|みせ}を{出|だ}したって — "Got a pile of money and opened a shop, I hear." って marks it as hearsay. The record says the same thing formally: {出産後|しゅっさんご}、{彼女|かのじょ}の{親|おや}は{雑貨店|ざっかてん}を{開店|かいてん}した, with 開店.

=== add phrase.ja.必要とする | word | 必要とする (need, require)
## What it means
"To need, require": a formal verb phrase. Xを必要とする means "require X".

- この{仕事|しごと}は{経験|けいけん}を{必要|ひつよう}とする。 — This job requires experience.
- {君|きみ}を{必要|ひつよう}としている{人|ひと}がいる。 — There are people who need you.

## How it's built
{必要|ひつよう} "necessary" + と + する. This と is the "as" of X を Y とする "treat X as Y" (see that guide), so literally "treat X as necessary". It conjugates like する: 必要としている (is needing), 必要としない, 必要とされる (be needed).

## Where it fits
Formal, written, or deliberately weighty speech.

## Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- Xが{必要|ひつよう}だ: everyday "X is needed".
- Xが{要|い}る: casual "need".
- {欲|ほ}しい: "want", not "need".

## Nuance and register
Formal. In casual speech it sounds bookish, or mocking if said pointedly.

## Common mistakes
Marking the needed thing with が: it takes を (血を必要とする).

## Where you met it
{君|きみ}がいかに{処女|しょじょ}の{血|ち}を{必要|ひつよう}としていたのかが よーく{分|わ}かるよ — "I can see very clearly just how badly you needed virgin blood." 必要としていた: "had been needing". The formal phrase with いかに sounds deliberately theatrical and mocking.

=== add ja.to_suru | grammar | X を Y とする (treat X as Y)
## What it is
X を Y とする means "treat X as Y, take X as Y, set X as Y". と marks the role or status something is given. The same と appears in として, "as".

## How to make it
- Xを + noun / na-adjective + とする: {父|ちち}を{手本|てほん}とする — take my father as a model; {血|ち}を{必要|ひつよう}とする — need blood
- Declaring: {会議|かいぎ}は{中止|ちゅうし}とする。 — The meeting is hereby cancelled.
- Supposing (after a clause + だ): {仮|かり}にそれが{本当|ほんとう}だとする。 — Suppose that's true.
- として, "as": {先生|せんせい}として{働|はたら}く — work as a teacher

## When to use it
Formal statements, rules, plans, definitions, and set phrases such as 必要とする, {目標|もくひょう}とする (aim for), {問題|もんだい}とする (treat as a problem).

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- にする: "decide on, make into": コーヒーにする (I'll have coffee). とする is more formal and is about status or definition.
- と{思|おも}う / と{言|い}う: the quoting と, a different job.

## Nuance and register
Formal, written, official.

## Common mistakes
Reading this と as "and" or as the quoting と.

## Where you met it
In {処女|しょじょ}の{血|ち}を{必要|ひつよう}としていた, the と of 必要とする: "had been treating virgin blood as necessary", that is, "had needed it".

=== add ja.ikani_ka | grammar | いかに…か (just how much…)
## What it is
いかに "how (very)" with a clause ending in か or のか means "just how much / how very …". It stresses degree, usually inside a bigger sentence ("I understand how…", "it shows how…"). It's the formal cousin of どんなに and どれほど.

## How to make it
いかに + clause + か / のか / ことか:
- いかに{大切|たいせつ}か{分|わ}かった。 — I realised how important it is.
- {彼|かれ}がいかに{苦労|くろう}したかを{示|しめ}している。 — It shows how much he struggled.
- As an exclamation: いかに{愚|おろ}かなことか。 — How foolish!
- With ～ても, "no matter how": いかに{努力|どりょく}しても — however hard you try

## When to use it
Embedded "how much" after verbs like {分|わ}かる, {知|し}る, {示|しめ}す; exclamations in speeches and writing.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Asking how to do something: どうやって or どのように (いかにして is literary).
- Casual speech: どれだけ, どんなに: どれだけ{好|す}きか{分|わ}かる?

## Nuance and register
Bookish and rhetorical. In casual speech it sounds theatrical or sarcastic.

## Common mistakes
Dropping the か: いかに大切が分かった ✗.

## Where you met it
{君|きみ}がいかに{処女|しょじょ}の{血|ち}を{必要|ひつよう}としていたのかが よーく{分|わ}かるよ. The いかに…のか clause is what's understood (が marks it for 分かる): "I see very clearly just how much you needed virgin blood." Bookish いかに next to casual よーく分かるよ makes it mocking.

=== add ja.ni_mieru | grammar | ～に見える (looks like, can pass for)
## What it is
～に見える (and ～く見える, ～ように見える) means "looks (like), appears, can pass for", judging by appearance.

## How to make it
- Noun + に見える: {学生|がくせい}に{見|み}える — looks like a student
- な-adjective + に見える: {元気|げんき}に{見|み}える — looks well
- い-adjective + く見える: {若|わか}く{見|み}える — looks young
- Clause + ように見える: {怒|おこ}っているように{見|み}える — looks as if he's angry

## When to use it
Judging by looks, especially when looks and reality may differ: {年|とし}より{若|わか}く{見|み}える — looks younger than (he is).

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- ～そうだ: an impression or prediction from appearance: おいしそう (looks tasty), {雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}りそう (looks like rain).
- ～らしい / ～ようだ: inference from evidence, not only looks.
- ～みたい: casual "seems like".

## Nuance and register
Neutral. Often paired with {見|み}た{目|め}は / 見た目じゃ to contrast looks with reality.

## Common mistakes
若いに見える ✗. い-adjectives take く: 若く見える.

## Where you met it
{見|み}た{目|め}じゃ まだまだ{十代|じゅうだい}に{見|み}えるがね — "By looks alone, she'd still pass for a teenager, though." 十代に見える: looks like someone in her teens. 見た目じゃ and the trailing がね hint that the reality is different.

=== add ja.nari | grammar | なり (in one's own way; within X's limits)
## What it is
X なり means "within X's own limits, in the way that fits X": whatever X can manage or sees as right, measured by X's standards rather than an outside one. That's why it's often modest ("in my own way") or quietly reserved ("by his standards").

## How to make it
- Noun + なりの + noun: {彼|かれ}なりの{理由|りゆう} — his own reasons
- Noun + なりに + verb: {私|わたし}なりに{頑張|がんば}った — I did my best in my own way
- X は X なりに: {子供|こども}は{子供|こども}なりに{考|かんが}えている — Kids think about it too, in their own way.
- After adjectives and verbs, "as far as that allows": {安|やす}いなりに{美味|おい}しい — tasty for the price; {分|わ}からないなりに{考|かんが}えた — I thought it through as best I could without knowing.

## When to use it
- Modesty about your own effort or opinion: {自分|じぶん}なりに{考|かんが}えてみました。
- Acknowledging someone else's effort or logic while implying its limits: {彼|かれ}なりに{努力|どりょく}している。 — He's trying, in his way.
- "In keeping with": {値段|ねだん}なりの{品質|ひんしつ} — quality that matches the price

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- {独自|どくじ}の: original, unique (a unique method), with no sense of limits.
- らしい: typical of: {彼|かれ}らしい{答|こた}え, an answer typical of him (his character, not his limits).
- {自分|じぶん}の: plain "my own".
- Other words spelled なり:
  - Verb + なり, "as soon as": {帰|かえ}るなり{寝|ね}た — went to bed as soon as he got home
  - なり…なり, "either… or": {電話|でんわ}するなりメールするなりして — call or email, either way

## Nuance and register
Neutral. About yourself it's modest. About others it can be generous or faintly condescending: it grants that their view makes sense from where they stand, without agreeing with it.

## Common mistakes
- Using it to mean "according to (rules)": use に{従|したが}って or どおり.
- Mixing up なりの (before a noun) and なりに (before a verb or adjective).

## Where you met it
{君|きみ}なりの{正当性|せいとうせい}を{述|の}べた{供述|きょうじゅつ}が{記録|きろく}されている — "There's a statement on record in which you set out your own justification." 君なりの正当性 is a justification by your standards: the detective acknowledges the reasoning exists without accepting it. In effect, "your own (so-called) justification".

=== add ja.toori | grammar | ～とおり / ～どおり (just as)
## What it is
～とおり (～どおり after a noun) means "just as, exactly as": what follows matches what was said, planned or expected.

## How to make it
- Verb (plain) + とおり: {言|い}うとおり, {言|い}ったとおり
- Noun + の + とおり: {説明|せつめい}のとおり
- Noun + どおり (no の): {予定|よてい}どおり, {計画|けいかく}どおり
- Before a verb: とおりに; before a noun: とおりの

## When to use it
- Conceding a point: {君|きみ}の{言|い}うとおりだ。 — You're right.
- Following instructions: {説明書|せつめいしょ}のとおりに{組|く}み{立|た}てた。 — assembled it exactly as the manual says
- As planned: {予定|よてい}どおり{終|お}わった。 — finished on schedule

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- ように: looser, "in the way, like": {言|い}われたようにした (did roughly as told) vs {言|い}われたとおりにした (did exactly as told).
- {通|とお}り as a noun means "street" ({大通|おおどお}り). Same kanji, different word.

## Nuance and register
Neutral. 言うとおり often concedes a point before a "but".

## Common mistakes
予定のどおり ✗. Either 予定のとおり or 予定どおり.

## Where you met it
{君|きみ}の{言|い}うとおり、これまでの{君|きみ}の{相手|あいて}は{少女|しょうじょ}ばかりだ — "Just as you say, the ones you've gone after so far have all been young girls." The detective concedes the point to set up what comes next.

=== add ja.mono_wa_mono | grammar | X ものは X (X is X, and that's that)
## What it is
X ものは X: "what's X is X". Repeating a word around ものは says something can't be changed, however you look at it, like English "rules are rules" or "impossible is impossible".

## How to make it
- な-adjective: {無理|むり}なものは{無理|むり}だ — impossible is impossible
- い-adjective: {高|たか}いものは{高|たか}い — expensive is expensive
- Feelings: {嫌|いや}なものは{嫌|いや}だ — I just don't like it, and that's that
- With nouns it's usually without もの: {規則|きそく}は{規則|きそく}だ — rules are rules
- Often with の or さ at the end: {無理|むり}なものは{無理|むり}なのさ

## When to use it
Refusing, accepting a hard fact, ending an argument.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- X は X で: "X in its own way": {子供|こども}は{子供|こども}で{大変|たいへん}だ — kids have it hard in their own way. A different meaning.
- もの on its own ("thing") and ～ものだ ("that's how things are", or nostalgia) are different patterns.

## Nuance and register
Blunt and final. With さ it sounds world-weary.

## Common mistakes
Dropping な after a な-adjective: 無理ものは ✗.

## Where you met it
{頑張|がんば}ったって {無理|むり}なものは{無理|むり}なのさ — "Try as you might, what's impossible is impossible." ～たって ("even if") plus X ものは X: no effort changes it. なのさ adds a resigned shrug.

=== section word.ja.もの | Where you met it
In {頑張|がんば}ったって {無理|むり}なものは{無理|むり}なのさ, もの is part of the pattern X ものは X, "what's impossible is impossible". See the guide to that pattern.

=== add ja.teki | grammar | ～的 (-ic, -like, in the role of)
## What it is
Noun + {的|てき} makes a な-adjective meaning "-ic, -al, -like, in the manner or role of".

## How to make it
- 的な + noun: {現実的|げんじつてき}な{考|かんが}え — a realistic idea
- 的に (adverb): {基本的|きほんてき}に — basically
- 的 + noun, as a compound: マドンナ{的存在|てきそんざい} — a madonna-like figure; {国際的|こくさいてき}{問題|もんだい}
- Casual: {私|わたし}{的|てき}には — personally, for me (slangy)

## When to use it
Abstract adjectives ({政治的|せいじてき}, {一般的|いっぱんてき}, {経済的|けいざいてき}), and "a kind of / in the role of" with nouns for people: マドンナ的, {兄貴|あにき}{的|てき} (big-brother-like).

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- ～らしい: genuinely typical of: {男|おとこ}らしい (manly).
- ～っぽい: casual "-ish", often less than ideal: {子供|こども}っぽい (childish).
- のような: plain "like a".

## Nuance and register
Fairly formal with abstract words. With nouns for people it means "a kind of, in the role of".

## Common mistakes
Attaching it to verbs (✗), and overusing 私的には in formal speech.

## Where you met it
{彼女|かのじょ}、そこの{男子生徒|だんしせいと}たちのマドンナ{的存在|てきそんざい}だったようだ — "It seems she was a kind of madonna to the boys there." マドンナ的存在: someone in the role of the adored beauty, not someone actually called that.

=== add ja.dewa_contrast | grammar | では / じゃ (by, in, as for: contrast)
## What it is
では (casually じゃ) is で + は. In your line it's the で of "by, in, in terms of" with は for contrast: "going by X (at least)". Two other では / じゃ are different words: ではない / じゃない "is not", and では / じゃあ at the start of a sentence, "well then".

## How to make it
- Noun + では / じゃ + clause (place, means or standard, with contrast): {日本|にほん}では — in Japan (as opposed to elsewhere); {見|み}た{目|め}じゃ — by looks (but…)
- Noun or な-adjective + ではない / じゃない (negative): {学生|がくせい}じゃない — not a student
- Start of a sentence: では / じゃ / じゃあ, "well then": じゃあ、{始|はじ}めよう。

## When to use it
- Limiting the scope: {僕|ぼく}の{知|し}る{限|かぎ}りでは — as far as I know
- Contrasting places or times: {昔|むかし}はともかく、{今|いま}では… — whatever it was before, nowadays…

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- には: に + は, "for / to (a target)": {私|わたし}には{分|わ}からない — it's beyond me.
- は alone: just the topic, without the "by / in" of で.

## Nuance and register
じゃ is casual, では neutral to formal.

## Common mistakes
Reading every じゃ as a negative. Check what follows: only じゃない is "not".

## Where you met it
{見|み}た{目|め}じゃ まだまだ{十代|じゅうだい}に{見|み}えるがね — "Going by looks, she'd still pass for a teenager, though." 見た目じゃ = 見た目では: by appearance, as opposed to her real age.

=== add ja.noun_modifying_clause | grammar | Clauses that describe a noun (殺した連中)
## What it is
A whole clause placed directly before a noun describes it, like an English "who / that / which" clause, but with no linking word and in front of the noun instead of after it.

## How to make it
- Verb (plain form, any tense): {俺|おれ}が{殺|ころ}した{連中|れんちゅう} — the guys I killed
- Negative: {要|い}らない{連中|れんちゅう} — guys nobody needs
- With an object inside: {吸血鬼|きゅうけつき}を{取|と}り{巻|ま}くコウモリ — bats that circle vampires
- Adjectives work the same way: {高|たか}い{店|みせ}, {静|しず}かな{店|みせ}; and nouns with の: {雨|あめ}の{日|ひ}
- Inside the clause, the subject takes が (or の), not は: {俺|おれ}が{殺|ころ}した{連中|れんちゅう} ✓, 俺は殺した連中 ✗
- Tense is relative to the main sentence: {明日|あした}{来|く}る{人|ひと} (people coming tomorrow), {昨日|きのう}{来|き}た{人|ひと} (people who came yesterday)
- Plain forms only inside (no ます / です): {私|わたし}が{書|か}いた{手紙|てがみ}です

## When to use it
Everywhere. Japanese uses it far more than English uses relative clauses, including for content: {彼|かれ}が{来|く}る{可能性|かのうせい} (the possibility that he comes).

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Long English "who" clauses often become two sentences in natural Japanese.
- The clause can never come after the noun.

## Nuance and register
Neutral. Several stacked clauses make sentences dense, which is common in formal writing.

## Common mistakes
は inside the clause; ます inside the clause; putting the clause after the noun.

## Where you met it
{俺|おれ}が{殺|ころ}した{連中|れんちゅう}は{要|い}らない{連中|れんちゅう}なんだよ — "The guys I killed are guys nobody needs, you know." Two clauses describe 連中: 俺が殺した ("that I killed") and 要らない ("not needed"). The same pattern as {吸血鬼|きゅうけつき}を{取|と}り{巻|ま}くコウモリ.
