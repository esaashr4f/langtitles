=== ja.te_form | te-form て/で
## What it is
The te-form is the "connecting" form of verbs, adjectives and です/だ. Its core idea is "…and (then / so / in this way)…": it leaves a thought open so that something else can attach to it: another clause, a helper verb (いる, しまう, ください…), or an unspoken request.

## How to make it
- Ichidan verbs (most verbs ending in -eru/-iru): replace る with て. {食|た}べる → {食|た}べて, {見|み}る → {見|み}て
- Godan verbs, by the last sound:
- う・つ・る → って: {買|か}う → {買|か}って, {待|ま}つ → {待|ま}って, {頑張|がんば}る → {頑張|がんば}って
- む・ぶ・ぬ → んで: {読|よ}む → {読|よ}んで, {遊|あそ}ぶ → {遊|あそ}んで, {死|し}ぬ → {死|し}んで
- く → いて, ぐ → いで: {書|か}く → {書|か}いて, {泳|およ}ぐ → {泳|およ}いで
- す → して: {話|はな}す → {話|はな}して
- Irregular: する → して, {来|く}る → {来|き}て, {行|い}く → {行|い}って (not {行|い}いて)
- い-adjectives: い → くて. {高|たか}い → {高|たか}くて, いい → よくて
- Nouns and な-adjectives: で. {学生|がくせい}で, {静|しず}かで
- Negative: ない → なくて ("not…, so") or ないで ("without…", "don't…").

## When to use it
- Linking actions in order: "did A and then B".
{朝|あさ}{起|お}きて、{顔|かお}を{洗|あら}った。 — I got up and washed my face.
- Giving a reason or cause, especially for feelings and results.
{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}って、{行|い}けなかった。 — It rained, so I couldn't go.
- Saying how something is done.
{歩|ある}いて{帰|かえ}る。 — I'll walk home.
- On its own at the end of a sentence, as a soft request or encouragement among friends and family: the ください is left out.
ちょっと{待|ま}って。 — Wait a second.
{頑張|がんば}って！ — Do your best! / Hang in there!
- Before helper verbs, which add their own meaning: ～ている (ongoing or resulting state), ～てしまう (completely / regrettably), ～てみる (try), ～てもいい (may), ～てください (please). Each of these has its own guide.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For "if" or "when", use a conditional (～たら, ～ば, ～と), not the te-form.
{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}ったら、{行|い}かない。 — If it rains, I won't go. (not {降|ふ}って)
- For a reason you want to state clearly or that leads to a request, opinion or command, ～から or ～ので is better: the te-form reason can't be followed by a request.
{寒|さむ}いから、{窓|まど}を{閉|し}めて。 — It's cold, so close the window. (not {寒|さむ}くて、{閉|し}めて)
- For listing actions as examples rather than in order, use ～たり～たり.

## Nuance and register
The bare te-form request (～て) is casual and warm; with strangers or superiors use ～てください or more polite forms. A te-form at the end of a sentence can also leave a reason or complaint hanging ("…and so…"), letting the listener fill in the rest. In fast speech ～ている becomes ～てる and ～てしまう becomes ～ちゃう.

## Common mistakes
- {行|い}きて → {行|い}って: 行く is the one く verb that takes って.
- Using the te-form of a godan verb as if it were ichidan: {帰|かえ}る is godan, so {帰|かえ}って, not {帰|かえ}て.
- Ending a polite sentence to a stranger with a bare ～て: add ください.

=== ja.te_iru | ～ている (ongoing action or resulting state)
## What it is
～ている describes a situation that continues over time: either an action in progress ("is doing") or the state left behind by an action that already happened ("has done / is in a state of"). Which one depends on the verb.

## How to make it
te-form + いる. {食|た}べている, {読|よ}んでいる, {結婚|けっこん}している. Polite: ～ています. Past: ～ていた. Negative: ～ていない.
In casual speech the い drops: {食|た}べてる, {読|よ}んでる, {知|し}ってる.

## When to use it
- Before a noun (often shortened to ～た): {眼鏡|めがね}をかけている{人|ひと} / {眼鏡|めがね}をかけた{人|ひと} — the person wearing glasses.
- An action in progress, with verbs that take time (eat, read, run, rain).
{今|いま}、ご{飯|はん}を{食|た}べている。 — I'm eating right now.
- A resulting state, with verbs of instant change (die, marry, open, arrive, know, wear).
{窓|まど}が{開|あ}いている。 — The window is open.
{彼|かれ}は{結婚|けっこん}している。 — He's married.
- Habits and repeated actions.
{毎朝|まいあさ}{走|はし}っている。 — I run every morning.
- Jobs and long-term situations.
{病院|びょういん}で{働|はたら}いている。 — I work at a hospital.
- Experience or record, often with past events.
その{本|ほん}はもう{読|よ}んでいる。 — I've already read that book.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- "I know" is {知|し}っている, but "I don't know" is {知|し}らない, not {知|し}っていない.
- For "I understand", use {分|わ}かる / {分|わ}かった, not {分|わ}かっている, unless you mean "I'm well aware (so stop telling me)".
- For a future plan, use the plain form, not ～ている: {明日|あした}{行|い}く — I'm going tomorrow.
- For something deliberately left in a state by someone, ～てある is used: {窓|まど}が{開|あ}けてある — The window has been (left) opened.

## Nuance and register
～てる is everyday spoken Japanese; ～ている is neutral and written. ～ています is polite. In anime, ～てる dominates. わかってる often carries irritation: "I know already!"

## Common mistakes
- Reading every ～ている as "-ing": {死|し}んでいる means "is dead", not "is dying" (that is {死|し}にかけている).
- Saying {結婚|けっこん}する for "I'm married": that means "I will marry".

=== ja.te_shimau | ～てしまう / ～ちゃう (completely; regrettably)
## What it is
～てしまう adds the sense that an action is done completely and can't be undone, often with regret, surprise or embarrassment: "end up doing", "go and do", "do by accident", "get it all done".

## How to make it
te-form + しまう (a godan verb). {食|た}べてしまう, {忘|わす}れてしまった.
Casual contractions: ～てしまう → ～ちゃう, ～でしまう → ～じゃう. {食|た}べちゃった, {死|し}んじゃう, {行|い}っちゃった. Rough or dialect: ～ちまう / ～じまう.

## When to use it
- Regret or an unwanted result.
{財布|さいふ}を{忘|わす}れちゃった。 — I (went and) forgot my wallet.
- Doing something by accident.
つい{言|い}っちゃった。 — It just slipped out.
- Finishing something completely.
{宿題|しゅくだい}を{全部|ぜんぶ}やってしまった。 — I got all my homework done.
- Something about to happen that the speaker fears or can't stop.
このままだと{死|し}んじゃうよ！ — If this keeps up, you'll die!

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For a plain past fact with no feeling about it, the ordinary past is enough: {昨日|きのう}{映画|えいが}を{見|み}た — I saw a movie yesterday.
- ～ちゃう is casual; in polite speech use ～てしまいました.

## Nuance and register
～ちゃう sounds soft, casual and a little cute; ～ちまう sounds rough and old-fashioned. Regret is the most common reading in dialogue, but context decides: やっちゃった can be "I messed up" or "I did it!".

## Common mistakes
- Treating ちゃう as the verb {違|ちが}う ("different", Kansai ちゃう): after a te-form stem it's always しまう.

=== ja.te_kudasai | ～てください (please do)
## What it is
A polite request: "please do…". It's the te-form plus ください, the request form of くださる ("give me", honorific).

## How to make it
te-form + ください. {待|ま}ってください, {見|み}てください. Negative request: ～ないでください ("please don't"). {触|さわ}らないでください.
More polite: ～てくださいませんか, ～ていただけますか. Casual: the bare te-form ({待|ま}って), or ～てくれ (rough, mostly male).

## When to use it
- Asking someone politely to do something.
ここに{名前|なまえ}を{書|か}いてください。 — Please write your name here.
- Instructions and directions.
まっすぐ{行|い}ってください。 — Please go straight ahead.
- Pleading, often repeated.
{助|たす}けてください！ — Please help me!

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- To ask a favour of a superior, ～てください can sound like an instruction; ～ていただけませんか is softer.
- To ask for a thing, use noun + をください: {水|みず}をください — Water, please.

## Nuance and register
Polite but direct. Between friends it sounds distant; they drop ください. ～てくれ is blunt or commanding, often from men or in tense scenes.

## Common mistakes
- Using ～てください to your boss to ask a favour (too direct).
- ないでください for "please don't", not ないてください.

=== ja.te_mo_ii | ～てもいい (may; it's okay to)
## What it is
"It's okay (even) if you do…": giving or asking permission. Literally te-form + も ("even") + いい ("good").

## How to make it
te-form + もいい. {入|はい}ってもいい, {食|た}べてもいいですか. Also ～てもいいよ, ～ても{大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}, ～ても{構|かま}わない (formal).
Negative: ～なくてもいい — "you don't have to".

## When to use it
- Asking permission.
ここに{座|すわ}ってもいい？ — Can I sit here?
- Giving permission.
{帰|かえ}ってもいいよ。 — You can go home.
- Offering, or saying something is acceptable.
{明日|あした}でもいいです。 — Tomorrow is fine too.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For ability ("can swim"), use the potential: {泳|およ}げる.
- For "must not", use ～てはいけない / ～ちゃだめ.

## Nuance and register
～てもいいですか is the standard polite way to ask. ～てもいいよ can sound generous or, with some tones, reluctant. ～てもいいけど hints at reservations.

## Common mistakes
- Answering a ～てもいいですか question with いいです alone can be read as "no thanks" in some contexts; どうぞ is clearer for "go ahead".

=== ja.te_wa_ikenai | ～てはいけない / ～ちゃだめ (must not)
## What it is
"You must not…": a prohibition. Literally "if you do…, it won't go / it's no good".

## How to make it
te-form + は + いけない / だめ / ならない. {入|はい}ってはいけない.
Casual contractions: ～ては → ～ちゃ, ～では → ～じゃ. {入|はい}っちゃだめ, {飲|の}んじゃいけない.

## When to use it
- Rules and prohibitions.
ここで{写真|しゃしん}を{撮|と}ってはいけません。 — You may not take photos here.
- Warnings and scolding.
{見|み}ちゃだめ！ — Don't look!

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For a strong direct command "Don't!", the prohibitive {見|み}るな is harsher; ～ないで is a softer request.
- For "don't have to", use ～なくてもいい.

## Nuance and register
～てはいけない is general and slightly formal; ～てはならない is written or solemn; ～ちゃだめ is casual and often said to children or close friends.

## Common mistakes
- Reading ちゃ as "tea": after a verb stem it's the contraction of ては.

=== ja.polite_masu | polite ～ます
## What it is
The polite verb ending. It doesn't change the meaning, only the politeness: it is how you speak to strangers, superiors and customers.

## How to make it
masu-stem + ます. Ichidan: drop る ({食|た}べる → {食|た}べます). Godan: final u-sound → i-sound ({書|か}く → {書|か}きます, {話|はな}す → {話|はな}します). する → します, {来|く}る → {来|き}ます.
- Past: ～ました. Negative: ～ません. Past negative: ～ませんでした.
- Let's: ～ましょう. Shall we: ～ましょうか. Won't you: ～ませんか.

## When to use it
- Talking to people you're not close to, or in public roles.
{明日|あした}{行|い}きます。 — I'll go tomorrow.
- Invitations.
{一緒|いっしょ}に{行|い}きませんか。 — Would you like to go together?

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Inside a sentence (before nouns, in quotes, in most subordinate clauses) the plain form is used even in polite speech: {私|わたし}が{書|か}いた{手紙|てがみ}です — It's a letter I wrote.
- With family and friends, ～ます sounds cold or sarcastic.

## Nuance and register
Polite but not humble or honorific. Switching from plain to ～ます between friends can signal anger or distance; switching the other way signals closeness.

## Common mistakes
- Putting ます inside noun-modifying clauses.
- Forgetting that the stem of godan verbs changes vowel ({飲|の}む → {飲|の}みます).

=== ja.past_ta | past ～た
## What it is
The plain past form: something happened or was the case. It can also mark that an action is complete, and, in speech, that you've just realised something.

## How to make it
Like the te-form, with た/だ instead of て/で. {食|た}べた, {読|よ}んだ, {書|か}いた, {行|い}った, した, {来|き}た.
- い-adjectives: い → かった. {高|たか}かった, よかった.
- Nouns and な-adjectives: だった. {学生|がくせい}だった. Polite: でした.

## When to use it
- Before a noun, describing it: {昨日|きのう}{見|み}た{映画|えいが} — the film I saw yesterday; {俺|おれ}が{殺|ころ}した{連中|れんちゅう} — the guys I killed (see the guide to clauses that describe a noun).
- Past events.
{昨日|きのう}{映画|えいが}を{見|み}た。 — I watched a film yesterday.
- Completion, including before a future event.
{終|お}わったら{電話|でんわ}して。 — Call me when you've finished.
- Sudden realisation or rediscovery.
あ、ここにあった！ — Oh, here it is!
- Sharp commands in some fixed phrases.
どいたどいた！ — Out of the way!

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- "Already" with ongoing states often uses ～ている: もう{結婚|けっこん}している.
- Polite speech uses ～ました.

## Nuance and register
Plain and neutral. よかった ("it was good") is used for relief: "Thank goodness."

## Common mistakes
- {行|い}いた → {行|い}った.
- Making な-adjectives past with かった: {静|しず}かだった, not {静|しず}かかった.

=== ja.negative_nai | negative ～ない
## What it is
The plain negative: "not". ない itself conjugates like an い-adjective, so it has its own past (なかった) and te-form (なくて).

## How to make it
- Ichidan: る → ない. {食|た}べない.
- Godan: final u-sound → a-sound + ない. {書|か}かない, {飲|の}まない. Verbs ending in う use わ: {買|か}う → {買|か}わない.
- する → しない, {来|く}る → {来|こ}ない, ある → ない.
- い-adjectives: くない. {高|たか}くない. Nouns: じゃない / ではない.
- Casual: ～ん ({知|し}らん), ～ねえ ({知|し}らねえ, rough).

## When to use it
- Before a noun, describing it: {要|い}らない{物|もの} — things nobody needs; {知|し}らない{人|ひと} — a stranger.
- Plain negation.
{今日|きょう}は{行|い}かない。 — I'm not going today.
- Questions as invitations: "won't you…?"
{一緒|いっしょ}に{行|い}かない？ — Want to come along?
- Before ～なければ, ～なくても, ～ないで.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- "There isn't" is ない, the negative of ある, not あらない.
- For "without doing", use ～ないで or ～ずに: {何|なに}も{言|い}わないで{出|で}て{行|い}った.

## Nuance and register
～ねえ is rough and masculine; ～ん is casual and common in older or regional speech. Rising ～ない？ is often an invitation, not a negative statement.

## Common mistakes
- {買|か}あない → {買|か}わない.
- {来|く}ない → {来|こ}ない.

=== ja.volitional | volitional ～よう / ～おう (let's; I'll)
## What it is
The form of intention: "let's…" with others, "I'll…" to yourself.

## How to make it
- Ichidan: る → よう. {食|た}べよう.
- Godan: final u-sound → o-sound + う. {行|い}く → {行|い}こう, {帰|かえ}る → {帰|かえ}ろう.
- する → しよう, {来|く}る → {来|こ}よう. Polite: ～ましょう.

## When to use it
- Suggesting doing something together.
{行|い}こう！ — Let's go!
- Deciding, said to yourself.
よし、{帰|かえ}ろう。 — Right, I'll head home.
- With と{思|おも}う: plans.
{医者|いしゃ}になろうと{思|おも}う。 — I'm thinking of becoming a doctor.
- With とする: "try to / be about to".
{逃|に}げようとした。 — He tried to escape.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For a firm decided plan, the plain form or ～つもり is used: {来年|らいねん}{留学|りゅうがく}するつもりだ.
- For "shall I?" offers, ～ましょうか.

## Nuance and register
Plain volitional is casual; ～ましょう is polite. だろう / でしょう look similar but mean "probably".

## Common mistakes
- Confusing {行|い}こう ("let's go") with the potential {行|い}ける ("can go").

=== ja.potential | potential (can do)
## What it is
The form for ability or possibility: "can", "be able to".

## How to make it
- Ichidan: る → られる. {食|た}べられる. In speech often ～れる: {食|た}べれる, {見|み}れる (common but considered non-standard).
- Godan: final u-sound → e-sound + る. {書|か}く → {書|か}ける, {話|はな}す → {話|はな}せる.
- する → できる, {来|く}る → {来|こ}られる / {来|こ}れる.
- The potential is itself an ichidan verb: {書|か}けない, {書|か}けた.
- The object can take が instead of を: {日本語|にほんご}が{話|はな}せる.

## When to use it
- Before a noun: {泳|およ}げる{人|ひと} — people who can swim.
- Ability.
{泳|およ}げる？ — Can you swim?
- Possibility in circumstances.
ここからは{海|うみ}が{見|み}られる。 — You can see the sea from here.
- Negative: can't, often emotional.
もう{我慢|がまん}できない！ — I can't stand it anymore!

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For permission ("may"), ～てもいい.
- Seeing and hearing that simply happen use {見|み}える / {聞|き}こえる ("be visible / audible"), not {見|み}られる.
- ～ことができる is an alternative, more formal.

## Nuance and register
The られる form of ichidan verbs looks identical to the passive and the honorific; context decides. Shortened ～れる (ら抜き) is everyday speech.

## Common mistakes
- {行|い}ける vs {行|い}こう: "can go" vs "let's go".
- Reading every られる as passive.

=== ja.passive | passive ～れる / ～られる (be done to)
## What it is
The passive: the subject has something done to it. Japanese also has a "suffering passive", where the subject is affected, usually badly, by someone else's action.

## How to make it
- Ichidan: る → られる. {見|み}られる.
- Godan: final u-sound → a-sound + れる. {書|か}く → {書|か}かれる, {言|い}う → {言|い}われる.
- する → される, {来|く}る → {来|こ}られる.
- The doer is marked with に (sometimes によって in writing).

## When to use it
- Before a noun: {盗|ぬす}まれた{財布|さいふ} — the stolen wallet.
- Ordinary passive.
{先生|せんせい}に{褒|ほ}められた。 — I was praised by the teacher.
- Suffering passive: being troubled by something, even with intransitive verbs.
{雨|あめ}に{降|ふ}られた。 — I got rained on.
{彼女|かのじょ}に{泣|な}かれて{困|こま}った。 — She cried on me and I didn't know what to do.
- Having something of yours affected.
{財布|さいふ}を{盗|ぬす}まれた。 — I had my wallet stolen.
- Respectful (honorific) use about superiors.
{社長|しゃちょう}が{来|こ}られました。 — The president has arrived.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- "My wallet was stolen" is usually said from your point of view: {財布|さいふ}を{盗|ぬす}まれた, not {財布|さいふ}が{盗|ぬす}まれた (which sounds like a news report).
- For "it has been decided / it's written", ～てある or ～ことになった is often more natural.

## Nuance and register
The suffering passive is very common in dialogue to complain. The respectful passive is a light honorific.

## Common mistakes
- {言|い}あれる → {言|い}われる (う verbs use わ).
- Marking the doer with から where に is natural.

=== ja.causative | causative ～せる / ～させる (make / let)
## What it is
The causative: someone makes or lets someone else do something. Whether it's "make" or "let" depends on context.

## How to make it
- Ichidan: る → させる. {食|た}べさせる.
- Godan: final u-sound → a-sound + せる. {行|い}く → {行|い}かせる, {待|ま}つ → {待|ま}たせる. Spoken short form: ～す ({行|い}かす).
- する → させる, {来|く}る → {来|こ}させる.
- The person made to act: に (let / ask) or を (make, with intransitive verbs).

## When to use it
- Before a noun: {子供|こども}に{食|た}べさせる{料理|りょうり} — food you give children to eat.
- Making someone do something.
{子供|こども}に{野菜|やさい}を{食|た}べさせた。 — I made my child eat vegetables.
- Letting someone do something.
{私|わたし}に{行|い}かせてください。 — Please let me go.
- Causing a feeling.
{母|はは}を{泣|な}かせた。 — I made my mother cry.
- Keeping someone waiting.
{待|ま}たせてごめん。 — Sorry to keep you waiting.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- To ask someone for a favour, ～てもらう is gentler than the causative.
- ～させていただく ("allow me to") is a humble set phrase, not a real request for permission.

## Nuance and register
～させてください ("let me…") is a common, earnest request. The causative toward superiors is rude; use ～ていただく.

## Common mistakes
- Using を vs に: に implies the person agreed; を (with intransitive verbs) implies they were forced.

=== ja.causative_passive | causative-passive ～させられる (be made to)
## What it is
The causative and the passive together: "be made to do (against my will)".

## How to make it
- Ichidan: る → させられる. {食|た}べさせられる.
- Godan: final u-sound → a-sound + せられる, usually shortened to a-sound + される. {行|い}く → {行|い}かされる, {待|ま}つ → {待|ま}たされる. (Verbs ending in す take only the long form: {話|はな}させられる.)
- する → させられる, {来|く}る → {来|こ}させられる.
- The person who forces: に.

## When to use it
- Being forced to do something.
{先輩|せんぱい}に{酒|さけ}を{飲|の}まされた。 — My senior made me drink.
- Being kept waiting, a very common complaint.
{一時間|いちじかん}も{待|ま}たされた。 — I was kept waiting a whole hour.
- Having a feeling brought out in you.
{考|かんが}えさせられる{映画|えいが}だ。 — It's a film that makes you think.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- If you chose to do it, just use the plain verb.
- ～させてもらう ("I was allowed to") is the grateful opposite.

## Nuance and register
Almost always a complaint or a feeling of being pushed. 考えさせられる is a set phrase, positive.

## Common mistakes
- Reading ～かされる as plain passive: {待|ま}たされる is "be made to wait", not "be waited for".

=== ja.imperative | imperative (command form)
## What it is
The blunt command form: "Do it!". It's strong and mostly heard from men, in emergencies, in sports, on signs, and in anime.

## How to make it
- Ichidan: る → ろ. {食|た}べろ, {見|み}ろ.
- Godan: final u-sound → e-sound. {行|い}け, {待|ま}て, {走|はし}れ.
- する → しろ (written: せよ), {来|く}る → {来|こ}い, くれる → くれ.
- Negative command: dictionary form + な. {行|い}くな — Don't go!

## When to use it
- Orders and shouts.
{逃|に}げろ！ — Run!
- Encouragement in sports or fights.
{行|い}け！ — Go!
- Quoting what someone told you.
{早|はや}く{寝|ね}ろと{言|い}われた。 — I was told to go to bed early.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Normal requests use ～て, ～てください or ～なさい (parent to child).
- ～な after a masu-stem (たべな) is a soft casual command, not a prohibition.

## Nuance and register
Rude or rough in everyday conversation, natural in emergencies and quotes. Women's speech tends toward ～て or ～なさい instead.

## Common mistakes
- Using it in requests to anyone you aren't very close to.

=== ja.conditional_ba | conditional ～ば (if)
## What it is
The "if" of conditions: when A is true, B follows. It focuses on the condition itself.

## How to make it
- Verbs: final u-sound → e-sound + ば. {行|い}く → {行|い}けば, {食|た}べる → {食|た}べれば, する → すれば, {来|く}る → {来|く}れば.
- い-adjectives: い → ければ. {安|やす}ければ, よければ.
- Negative: ない → なければ.
- Nouns / な-adjectives: なら or であれば.
- Casual: ～りゃ / ～きゃ. {行|い}かなきゃ, {見|み}りゃ分かる.

## When to use it
- General conditions and rules.
{春|はる}になれば{暖|あたた}かくなる。 — When spring comes, it gets warm.
- Advice: "you should".
{聞|き}けばいい。 — You should just ask.
- Wishes and regrets with よかった: "I wish".
{来|こ}なければよかった。 — I wish I hadn't come.
- Proportion: ～ば～ほど "the more…, the more…".
{練習|れんしゅう}すればするほど{上手|じょうず}になる。 — The more you practise, the better you get.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- A one-off past sequence ("when I went…, I found…") needs ～たら, not ～ば.
- If the second half is a request, order or intention and the first half is an action, ～たら is more natural: {着|つ}いたら{電話|でんわ}して.

## Nuance and register
Slightly formal or logical. ～なきゃ ("have to") is from ～なければ(ならない) with the end dropped.

## Common mistakes
- Using ～ば for past events that actually happened.

=== ja.conditional_tara | conditional ～たら (if; when; after)
## What it is
"If / when A happens, then B". The most versatile conditional in speech: it covers hypotheses, sequences, and surprising discoveries.

## How to make it
Past form + ら. {行|い}ったら, {食|た}べたら, {高|たか}かったら, {学生|がくせい}だったら, しなかったら.

## When to use it
- After A is done, then B.
{家|いえ}に{着|つ}いたら{連絡|れんらく}して。 — Get in touch when you're home.
- Hypothetical "if".
{雨|あめ}だったら{行|い}かない。 — If it rains, I won't go.
- Discovery in the past: "when I did A, I found B".
{窓|まど}を{開|あ}けたら、{雪|ゆき}が{降|ふ}っていた。 — When I opened the window, it was snowing.
- Suggestions: ～たらどう？ "Why don't you…?"
{休|やす}んだらどう？ — Why don't you rest?

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For a universal, automatic result (press this and it opens), ～と is typical.
- For "if that's the case (about something just said)", ～なら.

## Nuance and register
Everyday and casual-friendly. ～たら alone at the end, rising, is a suggestion: {行|い}ったら？ "Why not go?"

## Common mistakes
- Reading ～たら as past tense: the た is not past here.

=== ja.tai_want | ～たい (want to)
## What it is
"Want to do": the speaker's own desire to act.

## How to make it
masu-stem + たい. {食|た}べたい, {行|い}きたい. It conjugates like an い-adjective: {行|い}きたくない, {行|い}きたかった.
The object can take が or を: {水|みず}が{飲|の}みたい.

## When to use it
- Before a noun: {行|い}きたい{所|ところ} — a place I want to go.
- Your own wishes.
{家|いえ}に{帰|かえ}りたい。 — I want to go home.
- Asking close friends what they want.
{何|なに}が{食|た}べたい？ — What do you want to eat?
- Past wishes and regrets.
{会|あ}いたかった。 — I wanted to see you. / I missed you.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Other people's desires take ～たがる or ～たいそうだ / ～たいみたい: {彼|かれ}は{行|い}きたがっている.
- "I want (a thing)" is ほしい: {車|くるま}がほしい.
- "I want you to…" is ～てほしい.
- Asking a superior "do you want to…?" is rude; offer instead: いかがですか.

## Nuance and register
Direct; in polite speech ～たいです or softer ～たいと{思|おも}います. 会いたい is a very emotional line.

## Common mistakes
- {彼|かれ}は{行|い}きたい for "he wants to go".

=== ja.explanatory_n_da | ～んだ / ～のです (explaining)
## What it is
～んだ (～のだ, ～んです, ～の) presents the sentence as an explanation, a reason, or something the listener should understand about the situation. It adds "you see", "it's that…", or emotional emphasis.

## How to make it
Plain form + んだ / のだ. {行|い}くんだ, {行|い}ったんだ, {高|たか}いんだ. Nouns and な-adjectives take な: {学生|がくせい}なんだ, {好|す}きなんだ.
Polite: ～んです. Question: ～の？ / ～んですか. Feminine or soft: ～の.

## When to use it
- Giving a reason or background.
{頭|あたま}が{痛|いた}いんです。 — It's that I have a headache.
- Asking for an explanation of what you notice.
どうしたの？ — What's wrong? (what happened?)
- Emphasis, resolve or a strong feeling.
{僕|ぼく}がやるんだ！ — I'm the one who'll do it!
- Realising something.
そうなんだ。 — Oh, I see / Is that so.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For a plain fact with no explanatory angle, the plain form is better: overusing んです sounds pushy.
- Questions without ～の ask for information; with ～の they ask about something you've noticed.

## Nuance and register
Extremely common in speech. ～んだ can sound firm or masculine, ～の soft. A rising ～の？ is a gentle question.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting な after nouns: {学生|がくせい}なんだ, not {学生|がくせい}だんだ.

=== ja.particle_wa | topic particle は
## What it is
は marks the topic: what the sentence is about. "As for X, …". It also contrasts X with other things. Written は but read わ.

## How to make it
Noun (or phrase) + は. Can follow other particles: に は, で は, と は ("in", "at", "with" as topic). It replaces が and を.

## When to use it
- Setting the topic.
{私|わたし}は{学生|がくせい}です。 — I'm a student.
- Contrast.
{肉|にく}は{食|た}べるけど、{魚|さかな}は{食|た}べない。 — Meat I eat, but fish I don't.
- In negatives, often contrastive.
{今日|きょう}は{行|い}かない。 — Today I'm not going (another day, maybe).

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- New information and question words take が: {誰|だれ}が{来|き}た？ (not {誰|だれ}は).
- Inside a modifying clause, the subject takes が: {私|わたし}が{作|つく}ったケーキ.
- With ある / いる for something newly mentioned: {部屋|へや}に{猫|ねこ}がいる.

## Nuance and register
は points at what's known and focuses on what comes after it; が points at the noun itself. {僕|ぼく}は{行|い}く = "I'll go (as for me)"; {僕|ぼく}が{行|い}く = "I'm the one who'll go".

## Common mistakes
- Using は with question words.
- Repeating は in clauses inside a larger sentence.

=== ja.particle_ga | subject particle が
## What it is
が marks the subject, putting the focus on it: "X (and not something else) is the one that…". It also marks the object of likes, wants, abilities and senses.

## How to make it
Noun + が. In sentences with は already, が marks the thing described: {象|ぞう}は{鼻|はな}が{長|なが}い.

## When to use it
- New information, answers to "who / what".
{誰|だれ}が{来|き}たの？ — {田中|たなか}さんが{来|き}た。 — Who came? — Tanaka did.
- Something noticed or newly mentioned.
あ、{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}ってる。 — Oh, it's raining.
- Likes, dislikes, skills, wants.
{猫|ねこ}が{好|す}き。 — I like cats.
{日本語|にほんご}が{分|わ}かる。 — I understand Japanese.
- Subjects inside modifying clauses.
{母|はは}が{作|つく}った{料理|りょうり} — food my mother made
- が at the end of a clause means "but" (see the guide to けど / が).

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For the general topic or contrast, は.
- For direct objects of action verbs, を.

## Nuance and register
が emphasises the noun before it; は emphasises what follows. In speech, が is often dropped in casual questions.

## Common mistakes
- {猫|ねこ}を{好|す}き: 好き takes が.

=== ja.particle_wo | object particle を
## What it is
を marks the direct object: what the action is done to. Also the space you move through or leave.

## How to make it
Noun + を. Read お. Often dropped in casual speech: ご{飯|はん}{食|た}べた？

## When to use it
- Direct objects.
{本|ほん}を{読|よ}む。 — read a book
- The route or space moved through.
{公園|こうえん}を{歩|ある}く。 — walk through the park
{道|みち}を{渡|わた}る。 — cross the road
- The place you leave.
{家|いえ}を{出|で}る。 — leave home

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Likes, wants, abilities and understanding use が: {水|みず}が{欲|ほ}しい.
- Destinations use に or へ, not を.
- Meeting someone: {友達|ともだち}に{会|あ}う, not を.

## Nuance and register
Neutral. Dropping を is casual and very common.

## Common mistakes
- {友達|ともだち}を{会|あ}う → {友達|ともだち}に{会|あ}う.

=== ja.particle_ni | particle に
## What it is
に points to a target: a point in time, a place where something exists, a destination, a recipient, or a purpose.

## How to make it
Noun + に. Also masu-stem + に + verb of motion (purpose).

## When to use it
- Specific times.
{七時|しちじ}に{起|お}きる。 — get up at seven
- Where something is (with いる / ある).
{部屋|へや}に{猫|ねこ}がいる。 — There's a cat in the room.
- Destination.
{学校|がっこう}に{行|い}く。 — go to school
- Recipient or person acted upon.
{母|はは}に{手紙|てがみ}を{書|か}く。 — write a letter to my mother
- Doer in passive and causative sentences.
{先生|せんせい}に{叱|しか}られた。 — I was scolded by the teacher.
- Purpose of going or coming.
{買|か}い{物|もの}に{行|い}く。 — go shopping

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Where an action happens takes で: {図書館|としょかん}で{勉強|べんきょう}する.
- Relative times (today, tomorrow, every day) take no particle: {明日|あした}{行|い}く.

## Nuance and register
へ for direction is close to に but stresses the direction rather than arrival.

## Common mistakes
- {図書館|としょかん}に{勉強|べんきょう}する → で.
- {明日|あした}に{行|い}く → {明日|あした}{行|い}く.

=== ja.particle_de | particle で
## What it is
で marks the setting or means of an action: where it happens, what it's done with, why, or within what limits. After a noun it can also be the te-form of だ ("is…, and").

## How to make it
Noun + で.

## When to use it
- Place of an action.
{家|いえ}で{待|ま}ってる。 — I'll wait at home.
- Means or tool.
バスで{行|い}く。 — go by bus
{日本語|にほんご}で{話|はな}す。 — speak in Japanese
- Cause.
{風邪|かぜ}で{休|やす}んだ。 — I was off with a cold.
- Scope or total.
{全部|ぜんぶ}で{千円|せんえん}。 — 1000 yen in total.
{世界|せかい}で{一番|いちばん} — the best in the world

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- Where something simply exists takes に: {公園|こうえん}に{犬|いぬ}がいる.
- Destinations take に / へ.

## Nuance and register
Neutral. {学生|がくせい}で… ("is a student and…") is the copula, not the place particle.

## Common mistakes
- {部屋|へや}で{猫|ねこ}がいる → に.

=== ja.ending_yo | sentence ending よ
## What it is
よ at the end of a sentence tells the listener something you think they don't know or need to hear: "I'm telling you", "you know", "!".

## How to make it
Plain or polite sentence + よ. After nouns and な-adjectives, casual speech uses だよ (masculine or neutral) or よ / なのよ (feminine).

## When to use it
- Giving new information.
{明日|あした}は{休|やす}みだよ。 — Tomorrow's a day off, you know.
- Insisting or warning.
{危|あぶ}ないよ！ — Watch out!
- Urging, with commands and volitionals.
{早|はや}く{行|い}こうよ。 — Come on, let's go.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- When you expect the listener to agree, ね; for both, よね.
- Overusing よ to superiors can sound pushy.

## Nuance and register
Assertive but friendly. Rising よ is lighter; falling よ can sound annoyed. わよ and のよ sound feminine, ぜ / ぞ masculine and strong.

## Common mistakes
- Adding よ to every sentence: it implies the listener didn't know.

=== ja.ending_ne | sentence ending ね
## What it is
ね seeks or assumes shared feeling: "right?", "isn't it?", "huh". It invites the listener to agree, or softens a statement.

## How to make it
Sentence + ね. Casual after nouns: だね. Stretched: ねえ.

## When to use it
- Seeking agreement.
いい{天気|てんき}だね。 — Nice weather, isn't it?
- Confirming.
{明日|あした}{三時|さんじ}だね。 — It's three tomorrow, right?
- Softening.
ちょっと{難|むずか}しいですね。 — That's a bit difficult… (a polite no)

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- To tell someone something new, よ.
- よね checks what you believe you both know: {明日|あした}だよね？ "It's tomorrow, isn't it?"

## Nuance and register
Warm and very common. ねえ at the start of a sentence is "hey". Too many ね can sound childish.

## Common mistakes
- Using ね when giving information the listener couldn't know.

=== ja.though_kedo_ga | けど / が (but; although)
## What it is
Clause + けど or が: "…but…", "although…". Often it just softens or sets the background, without a real contrast.

## How to make it
Plain or polite form + けど / けれど / けれども / が. {行|い}きたいけど、{時間|じかん}がない.
が is more formal or written; けど is spoken. Casual: けどさ, だけど (at the start of a sentence).

## When to use it
- Contrast.
{高|たか}いけど、おいしい。 — It's expensive, but tasty.
- Background before a request or question.
すみません、{駅|えき}に{行|い}きたいんですけど…。 — Excuse me, I'm trying to get to the station…
- Trailing off to soften or hint.
{別|べつ}にいいけど。 — Well, it's fine, I guess…

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For "even though (and it's unexpected or frustrating)", のに is stronger.
- For "even if", ～ても.

## Nuance and register
A sentence ending in けど often hides a complaint or a request. が to start a sentence (が、…) is formal: "However…".

## Common mistakes
- Treating clause-final が as the subject particle.

=== ja.reason_kara_node | から / ので (because)
## What it is
Both mean "because, so". から states your reason directly; ので presents it as an objective cause, which sounds softer and more polite.

## How to make it
- から: plain or polite form + から. {寒|さむ}いから, {雨|あめ}だから.
- ので: plain form + ので; nouns and な-adjectives take なので. {雨|あめ}なので. Casual: んで.

## When to use it
- Reason, then result.
{疲|つか}れたから{寝|ね}る。 — I'm tired, so I'm going to bed.
- Polite excuses.
{用事|ようじ}があるので、{先|さき}に{失礼|しつれい}します。 — I have something to do, so I'll leave first.
- から at the end: answering why.
だって{好|す}きだから。 — Because I like it, that's why.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- から after a te-form means "after": {食|た}べてから{行|い}く.
- To a superior, ので is safer than から.

## Nuance and register
から is direct and can sound like an excuse or argument; ので is gentler. ～からね ("I'm telling you, …") can be a threat or a reassurance.

## Common mistakes
- {雨|あめ}ので → {雨|あめ}なので.

=== ja.darou_deshou | だろう / でしょう (probably; right?)
## What it is
Conjecture: "probably", "I suppose". With rising intonation, it asks for agreement: "right?", "isn't it?".

## How to make it
Plain form + だろう / でしょう. Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ: {雨|あめ}だろう, {静|しず}かでしょう. Casual: だろ, でしょ.

## When to use it
- Guessing.
{明日|あした}は{雨|あめ}だろう。 — It'll probably rain tomorrow.
- Seeking agreement, often with pressure.
{言|い}ったでしょ！ — I told you, didn't I!
- Wondering, with か.
{誰|だれ}だろう。 — I wonder who it is.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For less certainty, かもしれない. For strong expectation based on facts, はず.
- For "let's", the volitional ～よう, not だろう.

## Nuance and register
だろう is masculine or written; でしょう is polite and neutral; でしょ is casual and common among women and in arguments.

## Common mistakes
- {雨|あめ}だだろう → {雨|あめ}だろう.

=== ja.kamoshirenai | かもしれない (might)
## What it is
"Might", "maybe": a possibility the speaker isn't sure of, about 50% or less.

## How to make it
Plain form + かもしれない. Nouns and な-adjectives directly: {雨|あめ}かもしれない. Polite: かもしれません. Casual: かも.

## When to use it
- Possibility.
{遅|おく}れるかもしれない。 — I might be late.
- Softening an opinion.
そうかも。 — Maybe so.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For a stronger guess, だろう; for a near-certain expectation, はず.

## Nuance and register
かも alone is very casual and common among young speakers.

## Common mistakes
- Adding だ before かもしれない after nouns.

=== ja.nakereba_naranai | ～なければならない / ～なきゃ (must)
## What it is
"Must", "have to": literally "if (I) don't…, it won't do".

## How to make it
ない → なければならない / なければいけない / なくてはいけない. Casual: ～なきゃ, ～なくちゃ, ～ないと (with the rest left unsaid).
{行|い}かなきゃ, {勉強|べんきょう}しなくちゃ, {帰|かえ}らないと.

## When to use it
- Obligation.
{明日|あした}{早|はや}く{起|お}きなければならない。 — I have to get up early tomorrow.
- Telling yourself what to do.
もう{行|い}かなきゃ。 — I've got to go.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- For advice ("you'd better"), ～た{方|ほう}がいい.
- For "don't have to", ～なくてもいい.

## Nuance and register
ならない is formal or general; いけない is more personal; なきゃ / なくちゃ / ないと are everyday speech.

## Common mistakes
- Reading なきゃ as a negative: it means "must".

=== ja.particle_ni_wa | には (for / at, as for)
## What it is
には is に + は: the target or place of に, made into the topic or set in contrast. "For X, …", "at X (at least)", "to X (as opposed to others)".

## How to make it
Noun + には. {私|わたし}には, {東京|とうきょう}には, {明日|あした}までには ("by tomorrow, at the latest").

## When to use it
- Who something is possible, difficult or true for: {私|わたし}には{難|むずか}しい。 — It's hard for me.
{子供|こども}には{分|わ}からない。 — A child wouldn't understand.
- Where something exists, in contrast: {東京|とうきょう}には{海|うみ}がある。 — Tokyo (at least) has the sea.
- Purpose, with a verb: {駅|えき}に{行|い}くには、このバスが{便利|べんり}だ。 — To get to the station, this bus is handy.
- Giving to someone, set apart: {彼|かれ}には{言|い}わないで。 — Don't tell *him*.

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- では: で + は, for where an action happens or a standard: {学校|がっこう}では{静|しず}かだ (at school, he's quiet) vs {学校|がっこう}には{図書館|としょかん}がある (the school has a library).
- に alone: when there's no topic or contrast: {駅|えき}に{行|い}く.

## Nuance and register
Neutral. The は makes it sound like "as for X" or "for X, unlike others".

## Common mistakes
Using には for where an action happens (use では), and reading it as two unrelated particles.

=== ja.dewa_nai | ではない / じゃない (is not)
## What it is
ではない (spoken じゃない) is the negative of だ: "is not". It follows nouns and な-adjectives. The polite form is ではありません / じゃないです.

## How to make it
- Noun / な-adjective + ではない / じゃない: {学生|がくせい}じゃない, {静|しず}かではない
- Past: ではなかった / じゃなかった
- Casual contraction: じゃねえ (rough), じゃん is a different word ("isn't it!")
- い-adjectives don't use it: {高|たか}くない, not 高いじゃない (that means "it's expensive, isn't it!").

## When to use it
- Plain negation: {彼|かれ}は{医者|いしゃ}じゃない。 — He isn't a doctor.
- With の / ん, rhetorical: {君|きみ}のせいじゃないか。 — Isn't it your fault? (pointing it out)

## When it seems to fit but doesn't
- では as "by / in (contrast)": {日本|にほん}では… — in Japan. Only では followed by ない is "is not".
- じゃない at the end with rising intonation often means "isn't it?" / "you know", not a negative: いいじゃない。 — That's nice, isn't it?

## Nuance and register
ではない is neutral or written; じゃない is spoken.

## Common mistakes
Reading every じゃ as negative; check for ない after it.
