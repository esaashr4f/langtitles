# Shows for the future languages (section 17 of the build list)

Same idea as `corpus-shows.md`: subtitle files in the language itself, used to count words, grammar and phrases
so the most common items get guides written in advance. **List only: nothing is downloaded yet.**

Picked for popularity (the best-known titles at home, not only abroad) and for a spread of settings: family,
school, work, romance, crime, comedy, history. Films are included where TV alone can't give enough, or where
the film is the best-known thing in that language. **★ = start here** (everyday modern speech).

**Being honest about the small languages:** Russian, Arabic, Thai, Cantonese, Indonesian and Tagalog each
get a full 100; Vietnamese gets 79. Kazakh, Uzbek, Mongolian and Khmer don't have 100 titles that are both well
known and likely to have a subtitle file, so their lists are shorter and marked as such. I didn't pad them
with titles I couldn't stand behind. Their corpus will need YouTube auto-captions or Whisper transcripts
instead (see the note at the end).

**Where the subtitles are:**
- **Russian:** very good. OpenSubtitles and SubDL have Russian files for most of the films and many series;
  Kinopoisk/Okko/IVI originals often have Russian captions.
- **Arabic:** Arabic files exist, but they're usually **Modern Standard Arabic**, not the dialect being
  spoken. Netflix Arabic originals are the exception (dialect captions). Check a sample before counting.
- **Thai:** good for GMMTV series (official YouTube captions) and Netflix titles; lakorns are patchier.
- **Cantonese:** most HK subtitles are **standard written Chinese**, not spoken Cantonese. Files written in
  spoken Cantonese (粵語字幕) are rarer: worth looking for, otherwise transcribe.
- **Indonesian, Tagalog, Vietnamese:** films on Netflix / Prime / Viu usually have files; older TV series
  mostly don't.

(long) = hundreds of episodes: good everyday speech, but can come after the rest.

### How to fill the small languages later
Subtitle sites won't get Kazakh, Uzbek, Mongolian or Khmer anywhere near 100. The workable route is the TV
channels' own YouTube uploads: take YouTube's captions where they exist, otherwise transcribe the audio
(Whisper handles all four, Khmer least well). That's a separate tool from the subtitle downloader and isn't
built. It'll be decided when those languages actually start.
