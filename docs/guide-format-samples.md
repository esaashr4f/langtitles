# Guide format: samples to agree on before the library is written

Four samples: a common word, a grammar form, a Korean form and a set phrase. They follow the rules agreed so
far:
- a plain-English opening;
- written in general, never around one line;
- fresh example sentences;
- short where the item is simple;
- no romanisation.

The line-specific part is not in the guide. It's the free "In this line" note above the guide in the panel,
built from the line breakdown, shown at the end of each sample for comparison.

Mark anything to change: section names, order, length, tone, what to add or drop. The library is written in
whatever we settle on.

## Sample 1 · word · 言う (いう) "say"

### What it means
言う means "say": putting something into words, out loud or in writing. {言|い}ってよ！ "Say it!" Beyond
"say", it also names things ("X is called Y") and quotes what someone said.

- Saying something.
  {何|なに}て{言|い}ったの？ — What did you say?
- Calling or naming: XをYと言う "call X Y".
  これは{日本語|にほんご}で{何|なん}て{言|い}うの？ — What's this called in Japanese?
- Telling someone off or giving an order: 〜に言う.
  {先生|せんせい}に{言|い}われた。 — I got told (off) by the teacher.

### How it's built
A plain verb ending in う. In speech it's pronounced ゆう; it's always written いう.
Its forms go like {買|か}う: {言|い}わない, {言|い}って, {言|い}った.
The polite form is {言|い}います.

### Where it fits
- Everyday speech, any level of politeness.
- Quoting: 「{行|い}く」って{言|い}ってた "she said she'd go". って replaces と in casual speech.
- Inside set phrases: と{言|い}うか "or rather", と{言|い}っても "even so", {言|い}うまでもない "it goes without saying".

### Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- Speaking to someone older or a customer: おっしゃる (respectful, for what *they* say) and {申|もう}す
  (humble, for what *you* say).
  {社長|しゃちょう}がおっしゃいました。 — The president said so.
- "Talk" or "chat" as an activity: {話|はな}す, not 言う.
  {友達|ともだち}と{話|はな}した。 — I talked with a friend.
- "Tell" as in inform: {伝|つた}える or {教|おし}える.
  {住所|じゅうしょ}を{教|おし}えて。 — Tell me your address.

### Nuance and register
Neutral and everywhere. Repeated in anger ({言|い}ったでしょ！ "I told you!") it carries blame.

### Common mistakes
- 言う for "talk with": {話|はな}す.
- Writing ゆう because that's how it sounds: it's written いう.

> **In this line** (free, from the breakdown, e.g. on 「{嫌|いや}だって{言|い}ってるでしょ」): {言|い}ってる =
> {言|い}っている, "keep saying": the speaker has said it more than once.

## Sample 2 · grammar · ～ている

### What it is
～ている describes how things are right now: an action that's going on (食べている "is eating") or a
state that's resulted from something (結婚している "is married").

Which of the two depends on the verb. Actions that take time give "is doing"; changes that happen in an
instant give "has done and still is".

### How to make it
- The verb's て-form + いる.
  {食|た}べる → {食|た}べている
  {読|よ}む → {読|よ}んでいる
- In speech the い drops: {食|た}べてる, {読|よ}んでる.
- Past: ～ていた ("was doing").
  Negative: ～ていない ("isn't doing" / "hasn't yet").
  Polite: ～ています.

### When to use it
- An action in progress.
  {今|いま}、{電話|でんわ}している。 — I'm on the phone right now.
- A state left by a change: verbs like {結婚|けっこん}する, {知|し}る, {住|す}む, {来|く}る, {死|し}ぬ.
  {窓|まど}が{開|あ}いている。 — The window is open.
- Habits and repeated actions.
  {毎朝|まいあさ}{走|はし}っている。 — I run every morning.
- "Haven't yet" with まだ + ～ていない.
  まだ{食|た}べていない。 — I haven't eaten yet.

### When it seems to fit but doesn't
- "I know" is {知|し}っている, but "I don't know" is {知|し}らない, not {知|し}っていない.
- Plans for the future: the plain form, not ～ている.
  {明日|あした}{行|い}く。 — I'm going tomorrow.

### Nuance and register
Neutral. ～てる is the everyday spoken form; ～ている sounds careful or written.

### Common mistakes
- {死|し}んでいる read as "is dying": it means "is dead". "Is dying" is {死|し}にかけている.
- Forgetting that {来|き}ている means "has come (and is here)", not "is coming".

> **In this line** (free, from the breakdown, e.g. on 「{何|なに}してるの？」): してる = している, "doing
> right now".

## Sample 3 · Korean grammar · -아/어 주다 (doing something for someone)

### What it is
-아/어 주다 adds "for someone": the action is a favour. As a request it means "please do it (for me)":
해 줘 (casual), 해 주세요 (polite).

### How to make it
- The verb's -아/어 form + 주다:
  하다 → 해 주다
  기다리다 → 기다려 주다
  돕다 → 도와주다
- Requests: 해 줘 (casual), 해 주세요 (polite), 해 주시겠어요? (very polite).
- Doing it for someone you honour: -아/어 드리다.
  도와 드릴게요. — I'll help you.

### When to use it
- Asking for something.
  잠깐만 기다려 줘. — Wait for me a second.
- Offering or promising a favour.
  내가 사 줄게. — I'll buy it for you.
- Thanking someone for a favour.
  와 줘서 고마워. — Thanks for coming.

### When it seems to fit but doesn't
- A plain statement or a bare command, with no favour in it: just the verb.
  기다려! — Wait!
  (Compare 기다려 줘 — "Wait for me, please.")
- Doing something for someone older or senior: -아/어 드리다, not 주다.

### Nuance and register
It softens commands into requests. In songs, 줘 at the end of a line usually turns it into a plea.

### Common mistakes
- 주다 for favours to someone senior (드리다).
- Reading 해 줘 as two separate ideas; the 줘 *is* the favour.

> **In this line** (free, from the breakdown, e.g. on 「나를 구해 줘」): 구해 줘 = 구하다 "save" + 주다:
> "save me (please)".

## Sample 4 · set phrase · 気になる (きになる)

### What it means
気になる: something keeps catching your attention: you're curious about it, bothered by it, or
interested in someone. The feeling that you can't stop thinking about it.

- Curious, want to know.
  {結末|けつまつ}が{気|き}になる。 — I really want to know how it ends.
- Bothered, can't let it go.
  {音|おと}が{気|き}になって{寝|ね}られない。 — The noise is bugging me and I can't sleep.
- Interested in a person (often a crush).
  {気|き}になる{人|ひと}がいる。 — There's someone I've got my eye on.

### How it's built
{気|き} "mind, attention" + になる "become": literally "it becomes (a thing on) my mind". The thing goes
before が; the person who feels it is usually unstated or marked with は.

### Where it doesn't fit, and what to use instead
- Worry about something serious: {心配|しんぱい}する.
  {母|はは}の{体調|たいちょう}が{心配|しんぱい}だ。 — I'm worried about my mother's health.
- Clearly "like" someone: {好|す}き.
  気になる is earlier and vaguer than {好|す}き.
- Paying attention on purpose: {気|き}にする ("care about, mind"), which is about *your* choice.
  {気|き}にしないで。 — Don't worry about it.

### Common mistakes
- Mixing up 気になる (it draws my attention) and {気|き}にする (I choose to mind it).

> **In this line** (free, from the breakdown, e.g. on 「なんか{気|き}になるんだよね」): {気|き}になる here:
> something about it bothers them and they can't quite say what.
