# Shows and songs for the built-in guide library (section 15 of the build list)

Purpose: subtitle files to count words, grammar and phrases across lots of real dialogue, so the most common
items get general guides written in advance. Chosen for **popularity** (MyAnimeList "most popular", MyDramaList
popularity and top-rated lists, checked 24 Nov 2026) and for **coverage**: a spread of genres and settings
(school, work, family, romance, crime, medical, legal, historical, fantasy, comedy, sport) so the vocabulary
isn't all swords and spells. Get every season of each.

**★ = start here.** Everyday modern speech in many situations; these give the most common vocabulary first.
After the ★ titles, new words thin out quickly, and later files mostly add new forms of known words, which is
expected and fine.

**Where the subtitles are** (in the language itself, not English):
- **Anime:** Jimaku (it includes the Kitsunekko archive), at most 20 downloads a minute (Jimaku allows 25).
  Coverage is very good for everything on the list.
- **Japanese live action:** Jimaku has some; OpenSubtitles and SubDL have most Netflix-carried titles.
- **Korean dramas:** Korean subtitles exist mostly for titles on Netflix (Korean closed captions) and on
  OpenSubtitles / SubDL; older broadcast-only dramas are patchier.
- **Chinese dramas:** Chinese subtitles are common on OpenSubtitles / SubDL for Netflix, iQIYI and WeTV titles.
- **Variety shows:** Korean and Chinese variety shows almost never have dialogue subtitle files; their captions
  are burned into the picture. Netflix-made reality shows do have proper subtitle files, so those lead the
  "other shows" lists.

Long-running shows (hundreds of episodes) are marked **(long)**: worth it for everyday speech, but they can be
added after the rest.
